How It Is Worked Out

The same method most UK universities use, kept simple and transparent.

1

Average each year

Each year's average is weighted by credits, so a 30-credit dissertation counts twice as much as a 15-credit module.

2

Weight the years

Year averages are combined using your degree's weighting, for example one third for second year and two thirds for final year.

3

Solve for the rest

Modules without a mark yet are treated as one average, and Gradewise works out what it needs to be for 70, 60, 50 and 40.

Questions and Answers

What is Gradewise?

Gradewise is a free, open-source UK degree classification calculator. It works out your weighted average, your classification so far and the marks you need for each grade boundary, and can import your marks from Moodle.

How is a UK degree classification calculated?

Each counted year’s average is weighted by module credits, then the years are combined using your degree’s weighting, often one third for second year and two thirds for final year. A First usually needs 70% or more, a 2:1 60%, a 2:2 50% and a Third 40%.

What do I need to get a First?

Enter the marks you have so far in Gradewise and it shows the average you need across your remaining credits to reach 70%, the usual boundary for a First.

Does first year count towards my degree?

At most UK universities in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, first year does not count and only the second and final years do. In Scotland, classification is usually based on years 3 and 4. Always check your degree regulations.

Does a year abroad count towards my degree classification?

It depends on your university. Some leave the year abroad out, some count it at around 10%, and some classify on the final year alone. Gradewise has presets for each.

Can Gradewise use my marks from Moodle?

Yes. The Gradewise bookmark reads your assignment and Turnitin marks, weights and submission dates from Moodle, flags late work, and works out your classification in a panel on the page.
